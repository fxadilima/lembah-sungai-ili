# The Medieval Trading Colony of Tana (13th–15th Century)

In medieval history, Tana was a crucial Italian trading emporium located at the mouth of the Don River on the Sea of Azov (near modern-day Azov, Russia).

- The Setup: Established in the late 13th century, it was a joint but highly competitive "colony" shared by the maritime republics of Venice and Genoa. Both republics had their own distinct zones governed by an appointed consul.

- Commercial Importance: It served as the easternmost gateway of the Italian Mediterranean trading network. It connected Europe directly to the Silk Road via the Golden Horde. Italian merchants used Tana to export high-demand regional goods like fish, caviar, and Russian furs, alongside an intensive slave trade. Crucially for Venice, Tana was the primary source of premium hemp used to manufacture heavy naval ropes at the Venetian Arsenale.

- The Demise: The colony suffered immense strain during the Venetian-Genoese wars. It was later plundered by the Turkic-Mongol conqueror Timur in 1395 and was ultimately lost when the Ottoman Empire conquered the region in 1475.
