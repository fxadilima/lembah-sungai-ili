
# Ancient Indigenous Bear Cults

Before the arrival of the Bulgars or the Golden Horde, the Middle Volga region surrounding Kazan was inhabited by Finno-Ugrian and early Indo-European tribes (such as the Fat'yanovo and Volosovo cultures).

- The Bear Totem: To these ancient peoples, the brown bear was revered as a tribal totem, a master of the forest, and a sacred ancestor.

- Archaeological Finds: Excavations across the Volga and Kama river basins have uncovered ancient burial grounds containing bear teeth and claws wrapped in bronze mountings, used as holy protective amulets. Over centuries, this ancient reverence evolved into the folk custom of leading trained bears through settlements to ward off evil spirits.

## The True Symbol of Kazan: Zilant (The Winged Serpent)

While cities nearby (like Yaroslavl) adopted the bear as their crest, Kazan's true ancient lore centers on a mythical creature.

- The Dragon Legend: According to Tatar mythology, the hill where the Kazan Kremlin stands today was once infested with thousands of snakes ruled by a giant, winged dragon-serpent called Zilant.

- The Crest: The settlers cleared the hill using fire, and the legendary knight defeated Zilant. Over time, the creature's image transformed into a symbol of wisdom, power, and protection. It remains the official figure on the Coat of Arms of Kazan today.
